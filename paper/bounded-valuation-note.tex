\begin{abstract}
Fix a base $b\ge2$, a prime $p\mid b$, and a finite multiset of digits different
from $1$, while allowing arbitrarily many additional digits equal to $1$ and no
zero digits. We give two short proofs that the $p$-adic valuations of the
resulting integers are bounded. The first uses a finitely branching tree of
divisible suffixes and K\"onig's lemma; the second is an equivalent
$p$-adic compactness argument obtained by stabilizing digits from right to left.
In base ten with $p=2$, this gives a qualitative proof of Conjecture~2 of
Brier, Clavier, Gutsche, and Naccache. Fonga gives a quantitative proof of the conjecture,
obtaining explicit bounds and an effective finite procedure for
the even-target persistence program.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and context}

Let $b\ge2$. Fix nonnegative integers $m_d$ for
$d\in\{2,\dots,b-1\}$. Let $\mathcal A$ be the family of positive integers whose
base-$b$ expansions contain exactly $m_d$ copies of digit $d$ for each $d\ge2$,
contain no zero digit, and contain an arbitrary number of copies of digit $1$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $p$ be a prime divisor of $b$. Then
\[
\{v_p(n):n\in\mathcal A\}
\]
is bounded.
\end{theorem}

For $b=2$, the set of possible digits $2,\dots,b-1$ is empty, so the fixed
multiset is necessarily empty. Thus $\mathcal A$ consists only of binary
repunits $2^r-1$, all of which are odd. Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is immediate
in base $2$. We therefore assume $b\ge3$ in the substantive arguments below.

In decimal notation and for $p=2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is precisely the
qualitative boundedness assertion in Conjecture~2 of Brier, Clavier, Gutsche,
and Naccache \cite{BCGN}. Fonga gives a quantitative proof of the conjecture with
explicit bounds depending on the prescribed digit multiplicities and gives an
effective finite method for the even-target persistence program \cite{Fonga}.
The arguments below are instead short qualitative proofs based on compactness.

\section{Suffix-tree proof}

Assume for contradiction that $v_p$ is unbounded on $\mathcal A$.

For each integer $e\ge1$, choose $x\in\mathcal A$ satisfying
\[
v_p(x)\ge \left\lceil (e-1)\log_p b\right\rceil+1.
\]
Then $x\ge p^{v_p(x)}>b^{e-1}$, so $x$ has at least $e$ base-$b$ digits.
Moreover, since $p\mid b$, we have $\log_p b\ge1$, so the displayed lower bound
is at least $e$. Hence $p^e\mid x$.

Let $y_e$ be the suffix formed by the final $e$ base-$b$ digits of $x$.
Because $y_e\equiv x\pmod{b^e}$ and $p^e\mid b^e$, we have $p^e\mid y_e$.

Construct a rooted tree whose vertices at depth $e$ are the $e$-digit,
zero-free base-$b$ strings that use each digit $d\ge2$ at most $m_d$ times and
whose numerical values are divisible by $p^e$. The parent of a depth-$(e+1)$
vertex is obtained by deleting its most significant digit.

Parent closure is valid: if
\[
y_{e+1}=a_e b^e+y_e
\]
and $p^{e+1}\mid y_{e+1}$, then $p^e\mid a_e b^e$ because $p\mid b$, and
therefore $p^e\mid y_e$. After adjoining an empty root, the tree is finitely
branching and has a vertex at every depth. K\"onig's lemma therefore gives an
infinite branch.

Along this branch only finitely many digits can differ from $1$. Choose $N$
beyond the final non-$1$ digit, and let $A_N$ be the integer represented by the
first $N$ branch digits, counted from the least significant end. For every
$e>N$,
\[
y_e
 =A_N+\sum_{j=N}^{e-1}b^j
 =A_N+\frac{b^e-b^N}{b-1}.
\]
Since $p^e\mid y_e$ and $p^e\mid b^e$,
\[
p^e\mid (b-1)A_N-b^N
\]
for arbitrarily large $e$. A fixed nonzero integer cannot be divisible by
arbitrarily high powers of $p$, so
\[
(b-1)A_N=b^N.
\]
Reducing modulo $b-1$ gives $0\equiv1\pmod{b-1}$, impossible because $b\ge3$.
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \qed

\section{Equivalent $p$-adic compactness proof}

Suppose again that the valuations are unbounded. Choose
$x_k\in\mathcal A$ with $v_p(x_k)\to\infty$. Their base-$b$ lengths tend to
infinity because $x_k\ge p^{v_p(x_k)}$, and $x_k\to0$ in $\mathbb Q_p$.

Read digits from right to left. By diagonal subsequence selection, pass to a
subsequence for which each fixed digit position eventually stabilizes. Write
the limiting digits as $a_0,a_1,\dots$. The fixed budget of non-$1$ digits
implies that $a_j=1$ for all sufficiently large $j$. Since $p\mid b$, the series
\[
L=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_jb^j
\]
converges in $\mathbb Q_p$.

To justify convergence of the selected integers to $L$, fix $r\ge1$ and choose
$J$ with $p^r\mid b^J$. For all sufficiently large $k$, the first $J$ digits
of $x_k$ agree with $a_0,\dots,a_{J-1}$ and the length of $x_k$ exceeds $J$.
Thus
\[
x_k-L\in b^J\mathbb Z_p\subseteq p^r\mathbb Z_p.
\]
Hence $x_k\to L$. Since also $x_k\to0$, uniqueness of limits gives $L=0$.

For $N$ beyond the last non-$1$ limiting digit,
\[
0=L
 =A_N+\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}b^j
 =A_N-\frac{b^N}{b-1}.
\]
Here $p\nmid b-1$, so $b-1$ is a unit in $\mathbb Z_p$ and the geometric-series
identity is valid $p$-adically. Thus $(b-1)A_N=b^N$, contradicting reduction
modulo $b-1$ as above.

\section{Scope}

The arguments above are qualitative: they prove that a bound exists but do not
produce a computable optimal bound from the prescribed digit multiplicities.
Consequently, by themselves they do not supply the effective cutoff required
to execute the backward-search program for nonzero even terminal digits in
\cite{BCGN}. Fonga's quantitative theorem supplies that missing ingredient
\cite{Fonga}.

The result also does not prove that decimal multiplicative persistence is at
most $11$. It establishes only the fixed-digit-multiset boundedness statement.
