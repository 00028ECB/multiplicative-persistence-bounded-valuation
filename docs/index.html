Two Short Proofs of a Bounded-Valuation Theorem in Multiplicative Persistence

Jason Stewart

Published September 1, 2026 · doi:10.5281/zenodo.22544425

Abstract

Fix a base b ≥ 2, a prime p dividing b, and a finite multiset of digits different from 1, while allowing arbitrarily many additional digits equal to 1 and no zero digits. We give two short proofs that the p-adic valuations of the resulting integers are bounded. The first uses a finitely branching tree of divisible suffixes and König’s lemma; the second is an equivalent p-adic compactness argument obtained by stabilizing digits from right to left. In base ten with p = 2, this gives a qualitative proof of Conjecture 2 of Brier, Clavier, Gutsche, and Naccache. Fonga gives a quantitative proof of the conjecture, obtaining explicit bounds and an effective finite procedure for the even-target persistence program.
