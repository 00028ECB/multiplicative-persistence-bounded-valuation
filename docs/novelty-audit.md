## Result

No proof of the exact bounded-valuation statement was located in the searches completed through July 20, 2026. The directly matching source remains the 2021 preprint of Brier, Clavier, Gutsche, and Naccache. That paper labels the decimal statement Conjecture 2 and explicitly says it provides no proof, while giving a finite lemma for selected digit families.

This is evidence supporting possible novelty, not a guarantee. Citation indexes are incomplete; the proof may be folklore, unpublished, or expressed as a general compactness theorem under different terminology.

## Statement searched

Decimal form:

> Fix the number of occurrences of every digit 2 through 9, exclude zero, and allow arbitrarily many 1s. The 2-adic valuation of the resulting decimal integers is bounded.

General form:

> For base b >= 2 and prime p dividing b, fixing the finite multiset of non-1 digits while allowing arbitrary 1s gives bounded p-adic valuation. The b=2 case is trivial; the compactness argument handles b>=3.

## Adjacent literature considered

- Schlickewei, *Linear equations in integers with bounded sum of digits* (J. Number Theory, 1990): concerns finiteness for bounded digit sums in multiplicatively independent bases, not bounded valuation for a fixed digit multiset in one base.
- Medina--Moll--Rowland, *Periodicity in the p-adic valuation of a polynomial* (2015): a polynomial-value valuation dichotomy, not a digit-language theorem.
- Sobolewski, *On k-regularity of sequences of valuations and last non-zero digits* (2022/2023): studies valuations of p-adic analytic sequences, not the present fixed-digit family.
- Bloom--Croot, *Integers with small digits in multiple bases* (2025): digit-distribution existence in several bases, not valuation boundedness.
- Kominers, *A Fixed-Prime Criterion for Reciprocals in Missing-Digit Sets* (2026): concerns rational denominators and primes not dividing the base, structurally different from the present p | b integer problem.

None of these supplies the theorem or the suffix-tree argument found here.
